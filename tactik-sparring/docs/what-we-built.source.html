SummaryWhat exists today

TACTIK AI is decision rehearsal infrastructure. Executives negotiate live against a photorealistic replica carrying a quantified behavioural profile of a real counterparty, and receive an auditable, scored record of how they performed.

This document exists to make one thing unambiguous. This is not a concept deck. The behavioural engine, the adjudication layer, the scoring model, the database design and the commercial structure are built, tested and committed. What follows states precisely what is finished, what is not, and what is being asked for.

The position in four lines

Built and verified: the behavioural profile compiler, the live adjudication engine, the spoken-number extraction layer, the six-axis scoring model, the database schema and two security-critical backend services. Nineteen of nineteen extraction tests pass. Typecheck clean. A full negotiation runs end to end.

Not yet done: the reference implementation has not been merged into the live application, and live video has not been latency-tested against production.

Commercially designed: a ninety-day paid pilot with a single committed metric, a refund condition, a defined cohort and a one-page contractual exhibit.

The ask: one named introduction to a decision owner, or one room of them. Not a business-development retainer.

Section 01The idea, and the hard problem inside it

Most training products ask an executive to role-play against a chatbot. Ours puts them across the table from a counterparty modelled on someone real, then grades the encounter.

The engine already runs autonomous simulations between two replicas while an executive observes. The new capability inverts that: the executive becomes the counterparty. That change is commercially significant because observation is episodic, one deal and one invoice, while rehearsal is habitual and sells as seats.

It also introduced one genuine engineering problem, and solving it is the reason this is defensible rather than obvious.

Our design principle held that TACTIK must control the conversation: who speaks, when, for how long. That works when both sides are machines. Against a human it fails, because routing every reply through our servers adds two to three seconds and the counterparty stops feeling real. The pressure that makes the exercise valuable evaporates.

The resolution: TACTIK gave up generating the conversation and kept judging it. The video layer handles speech, fast and natural, including interruption. TACTIK observes every turn, extracts quantified offers, and writes an evidentiary record while the session is still running. The vendor decides what the counterparty says. TACTIK decides what it believes, what it will never agree to, and what it meant.

Two consequences follow. Video vendors become interchangeable, because scoring authority was never delegated. And the counterparty's reservation values never leave our database, so a participant who tries to talk the limit out of the replica reaches a character that knows its limits qualitatively and never numerically.

A note on the three extraction defects, because they illustrate the standard applied. Negotiators speak numbers messily. "Four ninety a box" means four dollars ninety. An early version read it as ninety-four. Another merged two sentences and read a figure as sixty-six point six. A third matched a short word inside a longer one. None of these produce an error message. They produce a confident, official-looking report that is wrong, which is worse than a crash. All three are now permanently pinned by tests.

Roleplay vendors have practice without a behavioural engine. Video vendors have rendering without behaviour. Producing that paragraph requires both, and the autonomous engine that makes it possible was already built for a different purpose.

Section 05The commercial design

A ninety-day paid pilot with one committed metric, measured against the customer's own numbers.

The metric: the rate at which the customer's team breaches limits it declared in advance, reported with the currency value of those breaches. The customer sets the limits. We measure against them. That removes the obvious objection, which is that a vendor is marking its own homework.

The commitment: baseline in two weeks, breach rate halved within ninety days. If it does not move, we extend at no cost or refund. The commitment is void if the team's agreement rate falls, so the metric cannot be improved by simply refusing to close.

What we refuse to commit to: margin on closed deals. In ninety days a price movement cannot be separated from market conditions, product mix or counterparty turnover. If it moves the wrong way for unrelated reasons we lose the account, and if it moves the right way we still cannot prove it was us. Accepting an unattributable metric is how pilots die, and declining the flattering number is more credible than accepting it.

Section 06What is being asked for

The constraint is not engineering capacity and it is not the product. It is access to one decision owner.

The next milestone is a single complete report generated from a real rehearsal with a real team. It runs in text, so it costs nothing to deliver. That report is what converts the ninety-day pilot from a proposal into a demonstration.

Either of these moves immediately

  • One named introduction, on success. Three to five organisations where you already hold a warm relationship with the decision owner. We take it from there and agree a success fee on the pilot and on the first year of seats.
  • One room. We run a live session at no cost for a group of founders or executives. One participant negotiates against a replica of a hard counterparty in front of the room, and everyone sees the scored report afterwards. You get programming that cannot be sourced elsewhere. Nothing changes hands.

What is not being sought

A monthly business-development retainer, outbound lead generation, or funded prospect research. Not as a judgment on those services, but because of sequence. Until the first report exists, an intermediary is selling a concept, and in a category with a small number of correct buyers a half-formed approach closes those doors for a year. The first customers are founder-led by design, because they are how the product gets defined.

The value of a connector is the relationship already held. That is what we are asking for, and we are willing to pay for it on results.
